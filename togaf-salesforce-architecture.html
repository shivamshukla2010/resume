What is TOGAF?

Enterprise Architecture, Applied to Salesforce

TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework) is the world's most widely used enterprise architecture methodology. When applied to Salesforce, it provides guardrails that prevent costly rework, sprawl, and misalignment between IT and business — problems that affect over 70% of CRM programmes.

Why TOGAF for Salesforce?

Salesforce is not just a CRM — it's a platform spanning Sales Cloud, Service Cloud, Marketing Cloud, Data Cloud, Agentforce, MuleSoft, and more. Without an EA framework, organisations accumulate technical debt, duplicate integrations, and shadow IT. TOGAF provides the shared language and process to govern the full Salesforce ecosystem, not just individual projects.

Core Framework

TOGAF's 14 Architecture Principles — Applied to Salesforce

TOGAF defines architecture principles across four categories: Business, Data, Application, and Technology. Below, each principle is mapped to concrete Salesforce practices, anti-patterns to avoid, and example guardrails.

Business Principles

🏢
BP-1 · Business
Primacy of Principles
"These principles apply to all organisations within the enterprise and any technology decisions must be consistent with them."
Salesforce Application

Establish a Salesforce Centre of Excellence (CoE) whose architectural decisions override individual project teams. All custom development, third-party apps on AppExchange, and Org mergers must pass through the CoE governance board.

Anti-pattern to Avoid
  • Project teams building bespoke Apex solutions without CoE sign-off
  • Business units buying separate Sales Cloud Orgs without consolidation review
🏛 CoE Governance Board 📋 Org Strategy Document
🎯
BP-2 · Business
Maximise Benefit to Enterprise
"Architecture decisions maximise the overall benefit to the enterprise, even if sub-optimal for a single business unit."
Salesforce Application

When evaluating Marketing Cloud Engagement vs. Marketing Cloud Account Engagement (MCAE/Pardot), choose based on enterprise-wide marketing strategy — not just one team's immediate need. Use Salesforce Optimizer and Health Check reports regularly.

Template — Business Case Criteria
  • Alignment with company-wide CX strategy
  • Cross-cloud reuse potential (e.g., shared Data Cloud segments)
  • Total Cost of Ownership across all Orgs
  • Impact on existing integration fabric (MuleSoft/API Gateway)
📊 Salesforce Optimizer
🔄
BP-3 · Business
Business Continuity
"Enterprise operations must be maintained in spite of system interruptions."
Salesforce Application

Salesforce provides 99.9%+ SLA but your customisations and integrations may not. Design for graceful degradation: offline-capable Lightning apps, MuleSoft retry policies, and bulk API fallbacks ensure business continuity.

Practical Guardrails
  • Use Platform Events for async integrations — decouples callers from Salesforce downtime
  • Store critical reference data locally for offline access (Mobile SDK)
  • Configure Salesforce Shield Backup and Recovery policies
  • Document RTO / RPO for each Cloud product in your Org
⚡ Platform Events 🛡 Salesforce Shield
⚙️
BP-4 · Business
IT Responsibility
"IT is responsible for owning and implementing IT processes and infrastructure, not individual business units."
Salesforce Application

Define clear RACI for the Salesforce Org — who owns metadata deployments, sandbox refresh schedules, release management, and AppExchange procurement. Use a Salesforce DevOps pipeline (Copado, Gearset, or SF CLI) owned by IT.

RACI Template
  • Responsible: Salesforce Admin / Developer
  • Accountable: Salesforce Architect / CoE Lead
  • Consulted: Business Product Owner
  • Informed: End Users, Compliance
🚀 DevOps Center

Data Principles

📦
DP-5 · Data
Data is an Asset
"Data is an asset that has value to the enterprise and is managed accordingly."
Salesforce Application

Treat every Salesforce object — Accounts, Contacts, Opportunities, Cases — as enterprise data assets with defined owners, quality standards, and retention policies. Use Salesforce Data Cloud (formerly CDP) as the unified customer data layer across all clouds.

Data Asset Registry Columns
  • Object Name → Data Owner → Sensitivity Classification
  • Retention Period → Masking Rules → Sharing Model
  • Source System → Integration Pattern → Last Audit Date
☁ Data Cloud 🗂 Salesforce Shield
👤
DP-6 · Data
Data is Shared
"Users have access to the data necessary to perform their duties; therefore, data is shared across enterprise functions."
Salesforce Application

Design Salesforce's Sharing Model (OWD, Roles, Sharing Rules, Teams) to enable need-to-know access without duplication. Use Connected Apps and Named Credentials for cross-system data sharing, not point-to-point exports.

Sharing Architecture Checklist
  • Define OWD (Private / Public Read / Public Read-Write) per object
  • Role hierarchy reflects reporting lines, not just access levels
  • Sharing rules documented with business justification
  • Periodic access reviews scheduled (quarterly recommended)
🔐 Sharing Model
📚
DP-7 · Data
Data is Accessible
"Data is accessible for users to perform their functions."
Salesforce Application

Use Salesforce Reports & Dashboards, CRM Analytics (formerly Tableau CRM / Einstein Analytics), and Data Cloud Query APIs to expose data in the context where users work — not in a separate BI system they have to context-switch to.

Access Patterns
  • Embedded CRM Analytics dashboards in Lightning pages
  • Report subscriptions for scheduled data delivery
  • Einstein Discovery for inline predictive insights
  • External Data Connectors for cross-system views
📊 CRM Analytics
🏷
DP-8 · Data
Data Trustee
"Each data element has a trustee accountable for data quality."
Salesforce Application

Assign data stewards for each key object (Account, Contact, Lead, Opportunity). Use Salesforce Duplicate Rules, Matching Rules, and third-party tools (D&B Hoovers, Ringlead, Cloudingo) to enforce quality at the point of entry.

Data Quality Framework
  • Completeness rules on required fields (validation rules)
  • Accuracy: matching rules linking Contacts to known email domains
  • Consistency: picklist value governance, no free-text
  • Timeliness: Last Modified Date SLA alerts via Process Builder / Flow
🔍 Duplicate Management

Application Principles

🧩
AP-9 · Application
Technology Independence
"Applications are independent of specific technology choices and therefore can operate on a variety of technology platforms."
Salesforce Application

Build Salesforce integrations using the REST/SOAP/Bulk/Streaming API contracts — not Org-specific internal endpoints. This ensures that when Salesforce changes their internal architecture (e.g., Genie → Data Cloud migration), your integration layer remains intact.

Integration Design Rules
  • Always use versioned API endpoints (v59.0, not latest)
  • Prefer MuleSoft or API-led connectivity over direct database calls
  • Externally-facing LWC components must work in both Classic and Aura if legacy exists
  • Use Named Credentials — never hardcode auth tokens in Apex
🔌 API-led Connectivity
🤝
AP-10 · Application
Ease of Use
"Applications are easy to use. The underlying technology is transparent to users, who can concentrate on tasks at hand."
Salesforce Application

Apply this to Lightning page design: each page layout should surface only the information the user needs for their role. Use Dynamic Forms, Conditional Visibility, and Screen Flows to guide users through complex processes without exposing backend complexity.

UX Guardrails
  • No more than 2 related lists per Lightning Record Page (use tabs)
  • Screen Flows for multi-step processes — never raw form fields
  • In-app guidance (Salesforce In-App Guidance tool) for onboarding
  • Mobile responsiveness mandatory for field-facing roles
⚡ Dynamic Forms 🎬 Screen Flows

Technology Principles

🔧
TP-11 · Technology
Requirements-Based Change
"Only in response to business needs are changes to applications and technology made."
Salesforce Application

Mandate that every change request to the Salesforce Org must trace back to a documented business requirement. Use a Change Management process: Dev → Staging → UAT → Production — no hotfixes direct to production, even for admins.

Change Control Template
  • Business Requirement ID (from Jira / Salesforce Cases)
  • Impact Analysis (objects, integrations, reports affected)
  • Rollback Plan (change sets, scratch org snapshots)
  • UAT Sign-off from Business Owner
📋 Change Sets / SF CLI
♻️
TP-12 · Technology
Responsive Change Management
"Changes to the enterprise information environment are implemented in a timely manner."
Salesforce Application

Salesforce releases 3 major updates per year (Spring, Summer, Winter). Establish an automated process to test each release in a Full Sandbox 60 days before the production push. Assign a Release Manager role within the CoE.

Release Readiness Checklist
  • Activate Salesforce Release Notes preview sandbox
  • Run automated test suites (target >85% code coverage)
  • Review critical updates — opt-in / opt-out decisions documented
  • Communicate impact to end users 2 weeks before rollout
🧪 Sandbox Strategy
🔌
TP-13 · Technology
Interoperability
"Software and hardware should conform to defined standards that promote interoperability for data, applications, and technology."
Salesforce Application

Salesforce's API-first approach aligns perfectly with this principle. Mandate REST/GraphQL API contracts over custom middleware, use Salesforce Connect for real-time external data, and publish API specifications to an internal developer portal.

Integration Standards
  • REST API for CRUD operations (JSON payload standard)
  • Bulk API 2.0 for data loads >10k records
  • Platform Events / Change Data Capture for event-driven patterns
  • MuleSoft Anypoint for complex orchestration
🌐 MuleSoft / Heroku Connect
🛡
TP-14 · Technology
Security & Privacy
"Information processing facilities must be consistent with enterprise security architecture and comply with applicable regulatory requirements."
Salesforce Application

Apply the principle of least privilege across all Salesforce profiles, permission sets, and permission set groups. Use Salesforce Shield (Event Monitoring, Platform Encryption, Field Audit Trail) for regulated industries (HIPAA, GDPR, CCPA).

Security Controls Checklist
  • MFA enabled for all users (mandatory since Feb 2022)
  • Platform Encryption on PII fields (SSN, DOB, credit card)
  • Event Monitoring for login anomalies and data exports
  • Connected App whitelist — no unapproved OAuth applications
  • GDPR: Right-to-be-forgotten implemented via Apex anonymisation
🔐 Salesforce Shield 🛡 Permission Sets

Governance Framework & Templates

TOGAF's Architecture Governance Framework defines how decisions are made, documented, and enforced. Here are practical templates for a Salesforce context.

🏛 Architecture Review Board (ARB)

  • Meets monthly (or at each major milestone)
  • Chaired by Enterprise Architect / CTO
  • Reviews new Cloud/Org additions
  • Approves AppExchange purchases >$10k ACV
  • Reviews deviations from architecture principles
  • Produces Architecture Decision Records (ADRs)
  • Monitors Org Health Score trends

📋 Architecture Decision Record (ADR)

  • Title: Short description of the decision
  • Date: YYYY-MM-DD
  • Status: Proposed / Accepted / Deprecated
  • Context: Why is this decision needed?
  • Decision: What was decided?
  • Consequences: What changes as a result?
  • Alternatives Considered: What was rejected & why?

🗺 Architecture Roadmap

  • Current State (as-is) architecture baseline
  • Target State (to-be) architecture vision
  • Gap Analysis: what needs to change
  • Work Packages mapped to Salesforce waves
  • Dependencies and sequencing
  • Estimated effort and resource requirements
  • Success metrics per wave

🔍 Architecture Compliance Review

  • Run at end of each Agile sprint
  • Check against 14 TOGAF principles
  • Review Apex Governor Limits usage
  • Verify no hardcoded IDs / credentials
  • Check bulk-safe Apex trigger patterns
  • Validate test coverage ≥ 85%
  • Issue Compliance Certificate or Waiver

📊 Org Health Monitoring

  • Salesforce Health Check score ≥ 80 target
  • API usage trending (approaching limits)
  • Record storage utilisation review
  • File storage and big object usage
  • Inactive users and licence waste audit
  • Deployment frequency and rollback rate
  • Custom code complexity metrics (PMD)

🚀 Release Governance

  • Salesforce release calendar subscribed
  • Sandbox refresh 60 days before production push
  • All automated tests pass in staging
  • Business stakeholder UAT sign-off obtained
  • Rollback plan documented and tested
  • Post-release monitoring window defined
  • Change advisory board (CAB) approval
⚠️

Common Governance Failures in Salesforce Projects

  • No sandbox strategy — developers testing in production
  • Profiles used as permission sets (creates upgrade debt)
  • Record Types overused — use Dynamic Forms instead
  • Flows and Process Builders running in parallel on same object
  • No Architecture Decision Records — tribal knowledge only
  • AppExchange apps installed without security review

Category 1 — TOGAF Fundamentals
1
What is TOGAF and what problem does it solve? Basic
▼
Answer

TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework) is a vendor-neutral enterprise architecture methodology and framework maintained by The Open Group. It provides a common language, process, and set of tools for designing, planning, implementing, and governing enterprise information technology architecture.

Problem it solves: Without a framework, large organisations build systems in silos — different departments make incompatible technology choices, data is duplicated, integrations proliferate, and change becomes expensive. TOGAF provides a structured approach to align IT investments with business strategy.

Without vs. With TOGAF
❌ Without TOGAF
  • Siloed system decisions
  • Duplicate data and integrations
  • No shared language across teams
  • Reactive, costly change
  • Shadow IT proliferation
✅ With TOGAF
  • Governed architecture decisions
  • Shared data models and standards
  • Common framework language
  • Proactive, structured change
  • Controlled technology portfolio

Salesforce context: A company running 3 separate Salesforce Orgs (one per region) without TOGAF governance ends up with 3 different data models, 3 sets of integrations, and no unified customer view. TOGAF gives the architecture board the structure to mandate a single-Org strategy and shared data standards.

Wave 1: Sales Cloud (Phases A–H). Wave 2 trigger: business wants 360° customer view → re-enter Phase A for Data Cloud integration → proceed through ADM again for the data architecture domain only.

What is the Architecture Content Framework (ACF) and what does it produce? Medium
▼
Answer

Explain the "Data is an Asset" principle and describe how Salesforce Data Cloud implements it. Medium
▼
Answer

DP-5: Data is an Asset states that data has economic value and must be managed with the same rigour as physical or financial assets — with defined ownership, quality standards, access controls, and lifecycle policies.

Data as an Asset — Five Pillars
👤 Ownership
Named data steward per object
✅ Quality
Validation rules, duplicate mgmt
🔒 Security
Field-level encryption, sharing rules
📋 Lifecycle
Retention, archiving, deletion policies
📊 Value
Analytics, AI predictions, reporting

Salesforce Data Cloud (formerly Customer Data Platform): is the native Salesforce implementation of this principle at an enterprise scale. It:

  • Ingests customer data from multiple Salesforce Clouds, external systems, and data lakes into a unified customer profile
  • Resolves identity using deterministic and probabilistic matching across data sources (one customer = one 360° record)
  • Governs data with consent management and data space segmentation (GDPR compliance)
  • Activates data for Marketing Cloud personalisation, Service Cloud case context, and Agentforce AI grounding
  • Measures value by connecting data to business outcomes via CRM Analytics dashboards
Architecture Implication

Without the "Data is an Asset" principle enforced, organisations let CRM data degrade: duplicate accounts, stale contacts, missing fields. Salesforce Health Check and Optimizer report on this — architects should set a minimum data quality score target (e.g., 80/100) in the architecture principles document.

TOGAF principles triggered:

  • TP-11 (Requirements-Based Change): Each field must trace to a documented requirement. Generic "might be useful" fields are rejected.

  • Document the decision in an ADR with justification and review date (fields not populated within 12 months are candidates for deletion).

What is an Architecture Decision Record (ADR) and write one for the choice between a single Salesforce Org vs. multiple Orgs? Advanced
▼
Answer

An Architecture Decision Record (ADR) is a short document that captures a significant architecture decision, its context, rationale, and consequences. It is part of TOGAF's governance artefacts and prevents revisiting the same decisions repeatedly.

ADR Template — Sample: Salesforce Org Strategy
ADR-001Salesforce Org Strategy — Single vs. Multi Date:2025-10-05 Status:Accepted Deciders:Enterprise Architect, CIO, Salesforce CoE Lead
Context:

The organisation has 3 regions (EMEA, APAC, Americas) each with independent Salesforce Org instances acquired through M&A. The business wants a single 360° customer view and unified reporting. Options evaluated: keep 3 Orgs, merge to 1 global Org, or use a hub-and-spoke with a central Data Cloud Org.

Decision:

Migrate to a single global Salesforce Org over 18 months. Use Salesforce Data Cloud as the interim unified customer view during migration. Regional customisation handled via Record Types and Permission Set Groups, not separate Orgs.

Rationale:
  • Aligns with BP-2 (Enterprise Benefit) — eliminates 3× licensing overhead and integration duplication
  • Aligns with DP-5 (Data is an Asset) — single Account-Contact model, no cross-Org data sync lag
  • Reduces total integration count from 47 (3 Orgs × ~15 each) to 17
Consequences:
  • 18-month migration programme required; business disruption risk during data consolidation
  • Regional admin autonomy reduced — must work within global CoE governance
  • Positive: single DevOps pipeline, unified reporting, lower AppExchange licensing
Alternatives Rejected:
  • Keep 3 Orgs: Rejected — cross-Org data sync is expensive, no unified customer view possible
  • Hub-and-spoke: Rejected — adds complexity without reducing licensing cost

How does TOGAF handle architecture compliance vs. full conformance, and when would you grant an Architecture Waiver? Advanced
▼
Answer

TOGAF defines a spectrum of architecture compliance from Irrelevant to Non-conformant. Not every project needs full conformance — the goal is controlled, documented deviation where justified.

Architecture Compliance Spectrum
Non-conformant
Violates principles; no waiver; must remediate
Consistent
Doesn't contradict; not explicitly consistent
Compliant
Meets all applicable principles
Fully Conformant
Implements all principles explicitly

Architecture Waiver — when to grant:

  • Legitimate business constraint: A legacy system cannot be changed in time for a compliance deadline — temporary deviation is granted with a sunset date
  • AppExchange package: A vetted ISV package doesn't conform to your coding standards but provides proven functionality. Waiver granted with condition: "must be reviewed at next major AppExchange version update"
  • Salesforce Platform Event: A team uses scheduled batch instead of Platform Events due to data volume constraints (100k events/hour limit). Waiver granted pending Data Cloud resolution

Waiver template must include: principle being waived, business justification, risk assessment, compensating controls, and expiry/review date.

Important

A waiver is not forgiveness — it is a documented, time-bounded exception with accountability. If waivers accumulate without review, it signals that your architecture principles are too strict and need recalibration.
